Buffett compares earnings with the 10-year Treasury yield; Graham’s size rule uses the CPI. Changes here also apply on the Score a Stock page.

How the weighting works: a market-value-weighted index owns the same slice of every company, so its share of each company’s sales, profits, debt and value adds up directly. The Dow is price-weighted, so it’s treated as owning one share of each company. Banks, insurers and real-estate trusts are left out of the scoring because their financial statements don’t fit these tests, and so are companies with fewer than 11 years of SEC data, so growth rates compare the same companies year to year; the remaining weights are scaled up to fill in. Share counts are total shares, not the “float” the index makers use. Slegers’ forecast points (16–18) aren’t scored here. Verdicts are rule-based readings of each investor’s published criteria and not financial advice. Member lists from Wikipedia; financial data from SEC filings; prices from Nasdaq.
